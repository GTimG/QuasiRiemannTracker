# Joint high-zero payment for a squared common label

**11 October 2026 — manual source component; stronger moment open.**
This pays the high-zero part of one genuine second-transform branch of
the pinned Cycle25 plain-moment proof. It retains the common label and
the child row together before counting either of them. It does not
prove a larger physical moment capacity or a new fixed strip. In
particular, arbitrary old moving twists and the low-zero child induction
remain obligations.

The source is Appendix B3, (B3.60)--(B3.72), of the
[pinned manuscript](https://github.com/HaileyStorm/QuasiRiemannTracker/blob/58344dfdbe756cf2f743da1908ffe0582418cf5b/public/proofs/cycle25-quartic-20261010/paper.tex).
Here the first common factors, first residual coprimality label and old
moving character width are all one or zero, respectively. The whole
first Gauss norm has the original two-plain coefficient, its canonical
slots and, when centered, its two equal-product rectangles. No new
coefficient restriction is imposed on that source.

## 1. An all-row consequence of the existing zero density

Write
\[
 R_0(d)=1-\frac{10d}{3(5-2d)},\qquad 7/10\le d\le3/4.
 \tag{JH.1}
\]
The unmarked specialization of (B0.40) counts sixth-power-free rows
whose buffered zero bin has parameter at least \(d\). For a fixed
finite group of ray characters supported on the original fixed bad
set, it gives \(H^{R_0(d)+\epsilon}(1+T)^J\), with the original
buffering and height costs. The same bound holds for **all** nonzero
element rows \(j\) with \(Nj\le H\), excluding the fixed-inducing
rows.

Indeed write uniquely \(j=k b^6\), with \(k\) sixth-power-free, retaining
the original primary-generator convention and its finite unit sectors.
The positive-real zeros of the primitive inducing character depend on
\(k\), while the primes dividing \(b\) remain natural Euler holes in
the displayed presentation. For every sufficiently small requested
\(\epsilon>0\),
\[
 \sum_{Nb\le H^{1/6}}
 (H/(Nb)^6)^{R_0(d)+\epsilon}
 \ll H^{R_0(d)+\epsilon},
 \tag{JH.2}
\]
since \(R_0(d)\ge R_0(3/4)=2/7>1/6\). Bounded residual scales are
paid directly. The deleted Euler factors cost a further arbitrarily
small power on the buffered positive half-plane, by (B0.14). They do
not remove any amplitude mask. A good-prime-free sixth-power-free
core is a fixed-inducing presentation and is excluded here; its
previously paid exceptional contribution is not recounted.

The finite ray character may vary among a fixed finite list. This
costs a fixed factor. This statement does not permit an arbitrary
moving ray conductor or an arbitrary character chosen separately at
each prime.

## 2. The actual squared-label branch and its normalization

Let the parent row width be \(m>0\), let
\(A=n_1+n_2+z\), and let \(a>0\) be the auxiliary amplification length.
It is distinct from every physical slot length. In the nominal clean
Gauss norm, \(K_0=2A-m\) and the enlarged row width is
\(K+g=K_0+6a\). Whole frequency dyads and their small support padding
are retained, as in B3.6--B3.8.

In the second complete-common-support extraction take
\[
 D_2=E_2=P,\qquad P\text{ squarefree},\qquad \log_Z NP=q,
 \qquad g_2=b_2=p_2=V=q,\quad t_2=0.
 \tag{JH.3}
\]
Every common prime has multiplicity one on both sides, and the genuine
frequency is in the nonunit partition at each such prime. Thus
\[
 j=P^2h',\qquad
 m'=m-6a-2q,\quad q'=q,\quad M'=m-6a-q,
 \qquad \Delta=a.
 \tag{JH.4}
\]
The corresponding child character is exactly a fixed finite ray
character times \(\chi_n(j)\), with every original canceled-prime
hole retained. If \(h'\) supplies valuation four at a common prime,
the resulting valuation six is its original zero extension, not the
constant one. The local scalar
\(F(P,P;P^2h')/(NP)\), restricted to its genuine partition and
extended by zero elsewhere, has modulus at most one by (B1.17).

The effective numeric row width is
\[
 \log_Z Nj\le m-6a+o(1).
 \tag{JH.5}
\]
At fixed nonzero \(j\), the possible labels satisfy \(P^2\mid j\), so
there are at most divisor-many \(P\)'s. Restricting to the declared
\(q\)-dyad only reduces this multiplicity. Hence JH.2 bounds the
**joint** high-zero population of \((P,h')\) by
\[
 Z^{(m-6a)R_0(d)+\epsilon}(1+T)^J.
 \tag{JH.6}
\]
An independent count of \(P\) is not extracted. In contrast, counting
each fixed moving twist \(\chi_{P^2}\) and subsequently multiplying
by \(Z^q\) does not give JH.6.

This agrees with the actual second-transform normalization. Excluding
the common-support **count**, the rows of the B3 ledger give
\[
 -A+(K+g-A)-a+q+(A-q)=K+g-A-a=A-m+5a.
 \tag{JH.7}
\]
The common correlation gain \(q\) and residual normalization loss
\(q\) cancel. The inner summed allowance is therefore
\(Z^{m-5a+\epsilon}\) when the original Gauss norm allowance is
\(Z^{A+\epsilon}\). It is the sum over actual labels and rows, not a
per-label allowance followed by an extra \(Z^q\) count.

## 3. Coefficients, the complete projector and pointwise payment

Keep the full residual coprimality projector of (B3.65), all canonical
slot coefficients, its Möbius signs and every original zero. Separate
the genuine full kernel by (B3.3), with its bounded weighted Fourier
measure outside the label and row sums. No arithmetic selector is
absorbed into that measure.

Allocate each divisor from the **complete** projector to both child
products as in (B3.65)--(B3.69). For a paired divisor dyad, its label
count and the two extraction scalars have aggregate exponent at most
\(2\theta_N\), by (B3.69). The actual post-allocation scales and slot
subcollections only shorten their whole products. On a buffered
high-zero bin, (B0.15) and (B0.16) therefore bound the paired centrally
normalized child product by
\[
 Z^{d(A-q)+\epsilon}(1+T)^J.
 \tag{JH.8}
\]
For two post-allocation reductions \(r_1,r_2\), pointwise powers
contribute the additional nonpositive exponent
\(-d(r_1+r_2)/2\); the divisor count is already paid by the two
central extraction scalars. Negative nominal scales use the source's
bounded clipping, not a growing support replacement. There are only
four rectangle pairs. Their independent norm heights and derivative
profiles remain in the original finite seminorm and height allowance.
They are not identified with a selected zero height.

The common mask may depend on \(P\) and the projector labels. This
does not change the primitive inducing character of \(j\). Its
polynomial-size Euler deletion has the original subpower cost. Finite
ray phases depending on the primary ray class of \(P\) are handled by
one fixed finite character list, chosen before the live labels.

Only after this exact allocation are signed weights bounded by their
moduli. Thus JH.8 does not drop the residual coprimality condition by
positivity, or assert the original correlation formula on artificially
noncoprime columns. Combining JH.6--8 pays the high-bin contribution
to this actual Gauss norm by
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 Z^{A-m+5a+(m-6a)R_0(d)+d(A-q)+\epsilon}(1+T)^J.
 \quad}
 \tag{JH.9}
\]
All finitely many prescribed profile derivatives have the same bound,
with their original finite seminorm allowances. Fourier tails and
buffer/height losses must fit into the requested \(\epsilon\).

## 4. A genuine high-bin reserve near the physical corner

For illustration use \(C=21/5\), require
\[
 n_1+n_2+Cz\le m,\qquad z\ge3m/100,
 \qquad 0<a\le m/1000.
 \tag{JH.10}
\]
Then \(A\le m-(16/5)z\). On \(7/10\le d\le3/4\),
\(R'_0(d)=-50/[3(5-2d)^2]\), so
\(R_0(d)+dA/m\) decreases throughout that interval. At its left
endpoint,
\[
 R_0(7/10)=19/54,\qquad
 1-19/54-(7/10)(1-48/500)=259/16875.
 \tag{JH.11}
\]
The excess of JH.9 over the original Gauss allowance \(A\) is at most
\[
 -\frac{259}{16875}m+a(5-6R_0(d))-dq+\epsilon
 \le-\frac{259}{16875}m+\frac{23}{7}a-dq+\epsilon.
 \tag{JH.12}
\]
If the **total** exponent loss is at most \(m/1000\), this is below
\(-m/100-dq\). The actual physical corner has
\(z/m\simeq .0357355\), so it lies strictly inside this example's
slot-ratio gate. This is a positive-power high-bin payment for the
specified branch, not a numerical near-equality exclusion.

## 5. What this component does not establish

The low-zero child moment at coefficient \(C=21/5\) has not been
proved. Repeated slot deletion can reduce \(z/M'\) below JH.10's
gate; a complete induction cannot silently retain that gate. Other
second-common multiplicities and every first-common/moving-twist
branch still require their own actual joint reconstruction. For
example, asymmetric first-common exponents can leave a fixed
\(\chi_{P^2}\) before this second transform; that extra label must
not be omitted from JH.5 or counted independently without its cost.

Consequently JH.9 cannot be inserted as a replacement for B3.40.
Complete physical Euler subsets, conductor deficits, supremum recovery,
principal terms and all three original function families remain part
of any proposed tracker theorem. This note proves one high-zero source
component and identifies the joint label bookkeeping that pays it.
No Lean, kernel acceptance or publication eligibility is claimed.

The subsequent [local physical moment candidate](LOCAL_PHYSICAL_MOMENT_EXTENSION.md)
uses a different one-step closure: low-zero children strip slots cheaply
and then invoke the existing global moment, while the generic parent
zero-moving-width high source is reindexed with all common labels.
That candidate has its own complete-source and independent-review
obligations; it is not asserted as a consequence of JH.9 alone.
