\begin{abstract}
We give a proposed refinement of the moment estimate in Collet's quartic
zero-free argument. Near the lengths used by the weighted numerator, the
coefficient of the prime-slot length is reduced from $9/2$ to $4.498$.
The proof separates rows according to their zero parameter: lower rows
are paid by removing slots, whereas higher rows are counted jointly
with their arithmetic labels. An exact rational certificate then gives
the boundary $0.8748866$ for the Eisenstein Hecke and ordinary Dirichlet
families. This paper presents the argument and its quantitative margins;
the accompanying supplement retains the full source-level proof,
including masks, transformed children, and the continuation argument.
\end{abstract}

\section{The result and the moment family}\label{sec:setting}

The starting point is the pinned Cycle25 argument of Collet~\cite{collet},
built on OpenAI's $7/8$ theorem~\cite{openai} and Levy's weighted
numerator~\cite{levy}. Its comparison boundary is
\[
 b_0=\frac{11}{12}-\frac{\ell_0}{4}
 =0.8748866470399641\ldots,
 \qquad
 927\ell_0^4-3135\ell_0^3+2433\ell_0^2+275\ell_0-100=0,
\]
where $\ell_0\in[1/6,1/5]$. We modify one local moment estimate in that
argument and leave the detector and weighted Euler expansion unchanged.

\begin{theorem}[Proposed endpoint, relative to the pinned framework]\label{thm:endpoint}
Put $\theta=4374433/5000000=0.8748866$. Every finite-order Hecke
$L$-function of trivial infinite type over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ is
zero-free in $\Re s>\theta$, with the principal pole at $s=1$ excluded.
The same conclusion holds for every ordinary Dirichlet $L$-function,
including the Riemann zeta function. Characters may be imprimitive and
have arbitrary conductor.
\end{theorem}

The local input is easiest to state in the Eisenstein ring
$\OO=\mathbb Z[e^{2\pi i/3}]$. Let $U=Z^M$, $Z>1$, $M>0$, and let $N$
denote the norm. In the natural family of the source, a row $k$ acts by
\[
 \psi_k(n)=\tau(n)\chi_n(k),
\]
where $\tau$ is a fixed finite ray character and $\chi_n(k)$ is the
sextic residue character with its natural zero extension.
There is no parent character of positive moving width. Write
$S_{k,j}(Z^{n_j})$ for the centrally normalized smooth ordinary character
sum on scale $Z^{n_j}$. A prime slot is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slot}
 Q_{k,i}=Z^{-z_i/2}\!\sum_{\mathfrak p\in\mathcal P_i^{\rm can}}
 \psi_k(\mathfrak p)\nu_i(\mathfrak p)
 W\!\left(\frac{N\mathfrak p}{Z^{z_i}}\right)
 \left(\frac{N\mathfrak p}{Z^{z_i}}\right)^{-\sigma_i+iv_i}.
\end{equation}
The pools $\mathcal P_i^{\rm can}$ are the source's canonical good-prime
pools. The slots share one smooth annular profile $W$, while their
allowed relative characters $\nu_i$ and real powers may differ, and their
heights are independent.
Set $z=\sum_i z_i$ and $Q_{k,I}=\prod_iQ_{k,i}$.

All natural zeros are retained. We also allow a common nonzero puncture
of norm at most $U^B$, independent of the summed row but possibly
dependent on frozen outer labels. A row is \emph{eligible} precisely
when it is not a fixed-inducing presentation in the source sense.
The exclusion ideal
\[
 Q_*=M_{\rm ray}\cap(72)\cap\mathfrak q_{\eta_0}
\]
is chosen with the fixed family and anchor $\eta_0$; it is proper,
nonzero, and stays fixed under transformations. This excludes all
principal twists within the actual parent translate of the allowed
$Q_*$-character group. It does not identify different parent translates.
Whole-character conjugation is handled by height reflection
(\supp{C}).

\begin{proposition}[Local physical moment]\label{prop:local}
Assume the incumbent bound $\beta_*\le7/8$ and its nontrivial branch
$\beta_*\ge51/100$. In the preceding fixed family, put $C=2249/500$.
If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window}
 .41M\le n_i\le .44M,\qquad .03M\le z\le .04M,
 \qquad n_1+n_2+Cz\le M,
\end{equation}
then, for every $\eps>0$, sufficiently fine canonical slots satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment}
 \sum_{k\ \mathrm{eligible}}
 |S_{k,1}(Z^{n_1})S_{k,2}(Z^{n_2})Q_{k,I}|^2
 \ll Z^{M+\eps}\mathcal H_J.
\end{equation}
The source radial weight is understood. Here $\mathcal H_J$ consists of
finite smooth seminorms and polynomial factors in all independent
heights. The estimate includes the four derivative-profile pairs used
for two-variable scale-supremum recovery.
\end{proposition}

The slot mesh is chosen from the requested loss and bounded geometric
ranges before the live arithmetic labels. For each fixed family and slot
system, profile orders, height orders, constants, and the eventual
threshold precede the live scales, masks, and heights. The descendants
are estimated by the existing $9/2$ and unrestricted zero-slot moments,
not by another application of Proposition~\ref{prop:local}. Sections
\ref{sec:low}--\ref{sec:high} give its proof, with the complete assembly
in \supp{G}.

\section{Rows with a smaller zero parameter}\label{sec:low}

Put
\[
 A=n_1+n_2+z,\qquad
 g=\pos{n_1+n_2+\tfrac92z-M},\qquad a=M/2000,\qquad \sigma=3a.
\]
The change of coefficient is only $9/2-C=1/500$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parent}
 g\le M/12500,
 \qquad .85M\le A\le44753M/50000<.9M.
\end{equation}
The auxiliary amplifier has prime norms on scale $Z^a$; its cost must
therefore be paid by a fixed positive margin, not absorbed as an
arbitrarily small loss.

After the two source transformations, classify nonexceptional rows by
a buffered zero parameter $\delta$, whose real zero location is
$(1+\delta)/2$. Assign every bin straddling $7/10$ to the lower branch.
Its upper endpoint is then $7/10$ plus a small buffer. On this branch,
a removed slot of width $v$ costs $(7/10)v$ in the squared-norm
exponent, before the reserved buffer losses. It releases $(9/2)v$ of the old moment's capacity.

For a canonical child of actual width $M_{\rm act}$ and clipped plain
lengths $x_1,x_2$, the capacity defect is
\[
 F_{\rm act}=\pos{x_1+x_2+\tfrac92\sum_i z_i-M_{\rm act}}.
\]
Greedily remove slots of total width at most $F_{\rm act}/(9/2)$ plus
one mesh interval. This either restores the old capacity or removes all
slots. The latter case uses the unrestricted zero-slot theorem, which
has no residual condition on $x_1+x_2$.

This payment is made \emph{before} removing the lower-row indicator.
The natural coefficient factorization and the nonnegative radial weight
give a positive sum of the remaining canonical products. Positivity
then enlarges it to the full old moment. Thus neither a discontinuous
zero-bin indicator nor an artificially altered coefficient is subjected
to a new Poisson transformation (\supp{F}).

The dyadic child width need not equal its nominal ledger width. If
$d=M_{\rm act}-M_{\rm nom}\le\eta_2$ and the nominal defect is at
most $F\ge0$, then $F_{\rm act}\le(F-d)_+$. For $0\le r\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:width}
 d+r(F-d)_+\le\eta_2+rF.
\end{equation}
Consequently a narrower actual state compensates for its larger defect.
Taking $r=(7/10)/(9/2)=7/45$, the transformed main child has nominal
defect at most $g+6a+\eta$ and source saving at least $a$. Before the
small losses, its reserve is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:low}
 a-\frac7{45}(6a+g)
 \ge\frac M{2000}-\frac7{45}
       \left(\frac{3M}{1000}+\frac M{10000}\right)
 =\frac{4M}{225000}>0.
\end{equation}
Choose the buffer, mesh overshoot, and support padding within this
reserve. Mask invariance of the zero maximum ensures that the
pointwise slot estimate applies to each actual allocated child.

\section{Higher rows: count the row and its labels together}\label{sec:high}

For $7/10\le\delta\le3/4$, the fixed-family unmarked density exponent is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
 \rho(\delta)=1-\frac{10\delta}{3(5-2\delta)},
 \qquad \frac27\le\rho(\delta)\le\frac{19}{54}.
\end{equation}
Here ``unmarked'' means that no positive prime-slot supply is required.
\supp{D} supplies the plain and inverse inputs for the fixed character
dictionary; \supp{C} identifies its natural representative, conductor,
and literal exclusion. The height-uniform density estimate holds on
all norm windows, including bounded windows.

To pass from sixth-power-free cores to all rows, write $w=ub^6$.
The factors introduced by $b$ are Euler holes: in $\Re s>0$ they do not
change zeros, since $1-\chi(\mathfrak p)(N\mathfrak p)^{-s}\ne0$.
At a common buffered height, retaining its polynomial height factor,
a core count $O(Y^{\rho+\eps})$ gives
\[
 \#\{w:Nw\le H\}
 \ll H^{\rho+\eps}\sum_b(Nb)^{-6(\rho+\eps)}
 \ll H^{\rho+\eps},\qquad H\ge1.
\]
One convergent ideal series with exponent $12/7$ dominates uniformly.

The essential point is to apply this count before freezing the first
arithmetic labels. To see which labels the row determines, write
$c_n(\pi)$ for the zero-extended sextic row and $q(\pi,n)$ for the
fixed reciprocity phase. For $1\le t\le5$,
\begin{align*}
 c_n(\pi)c_\pi(n)^t&=q(\pi,n)^t c_n(\pi)^{1+t},\\
 c_n(\pi)\overline{c_\pi(n)^t}
   &=q(\pi,n)^{6-t}c_n(\pi)^{7-t}.
\end{align*}
The two sides therefore have residues $1+t$ and $1-t$ modulo six.
When the exponent becomes six, the factor is the mask
$\mathbf1_{(n,\pi)=1}$, not one. Let $F$ be the product of the chosen primary prime generators to these
residues. In the second extraction, $G$ is the common gcd generator and
$V$ the forced nonunit radical. The genuine frequency is $GVh'$, and
the complete integer row is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row}
 w=u_0eFGVh',\qquad
 \log_Z Nw\le M-R-(c-d)_++L-6a+\eta,
\end{equation}
with a retained unit $u_0$ and $L=\log_Z NF$.
Here $c,d$ are first common-part widths, $R$ is active conductor width,
and $e$ is the extracted mask divisor. The exact identities are proved
in \supp{B}.

There are two different projectors. The first divides the second common
gcd $G$ and hence $w$. The second need not divide $w$: it stays fixed
outside the row sum, with its full signed allocation and paired
normalization. If allocation writes $n=dv$, the exact coefficient is
$\psi_w(d)\psi_w(v)$, even when the outside factor vanishes; the quotient
receives no new coprimality restriction.

Let $p$ be the first radical width, $E=\log_Z Ne$, and $R_0$ the width
of active primes whose row phase cancels. The first radical absent
from $w$ has width at most $p-R-E+R_0$. Once it and $w$ are fixed, all
other first and second labels are supported on their product. Rankin's
inequality gives the uniform reconstruction estimate
\[
 \#\{I:NI\le Z^T,\ \operatorname{rad}I\mid Q\}
 \ll_{B,T,\eps}Z^\eps\qquad(NQ\le Z^B).
\]
Indeed, bound the count by
$Z^{sT}\prod_{\mathfrak p\mid Q}(1-(N\mathfrak p)^{-s})^{-1}$,
choose $sT$ small, and absorb the large primes into an arbitrarily small
power of $NQ$. The finitely many small primes contribute a constant.
Only the missing radical costs its full norm exponent.

The actual smooth row ball has exponent
\[
 K_0+(d-c)_++6a+\varpi,\qquad K_0=2A-c-d+R+E-M.
\]
The positive padding $\varpi$ includes the first dyadic and fixed support
allowances. It contains every amplified row and shortens the dual range
in \eqref{eq:row}. If a fixed parent modulus has width $q$, write
$M=m+q$ and use $m$ in $K_0$; the final total-width ledger is unchanged.
A common Fourier majorant is chosen before the later labels and bins,
so the joint count preserves independent heights and the original
smooth seminorms (\supp{E}).

The resulting excess over the source joint allowance has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:high}
 \mathcal E=M(\rho-1)+\delta A+a(5-6\rho)
               +\mathcal E_1+\mathcal E_2+\eta,
\end{equation}
where the explicit common-part ledger gives
\[
 \mathcal E_1\le-\frac{79}{270}p-E-B_c,\qquad
 \mathcal E_2\le-\delta b_2-t_2,
 \qquad B_c=(3c-5d-R)_+/6.
\]
These inequalities use $L\le5(R-R_0)$ and retain every canceled prime
in the missing-radical count. The second widths satisfy $b_2,t_2\ge0$.
Finally $A/M\le113/125$, and direct differentiation of \eqref{eq:rho}
gives
\[
 1-\rho-\delta A/M\ge259/16875,\qquad 5-6\rho\le23/7.
\]
For $a=M/2000$ and $\eta\le M/1000$, \eqref{eq:high} is therefore
less than $-M/100$. This pays the higher rows with ample room for the
remaining small losses.

For completeness, the other source branches have independent reserves.
Comparison rectangles have old capacity at most $.93M+o(M)$.
For amplifier valuations $1,6,7$, use \eqref{eq:width} with $r=1/6$;
the smallest error reserve is $13M/120000$. The exact first exceptional
calculation gives $F_1\ge2c/3-4a-5\varpi/6$ and the second gives
$F_2\ge2b_2/3$. Their centered loss fits within $M-A>M/10$;
zero-frequency terms and the original amplifier errors do also.
No extra count of amplifier primes is introduced. These estimates,
with the exact normalizers and centered cancellation, complete
Proposition~\ref{prop:local}; see \supp{G} for the full ledger.

\section{Insertion into the physical numerator}\label{sec:physical}

The local estimate is needed only near one physical geometry. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geometry}
 \ell=\frac{626701}{3750000},\qquad
 h=\frac{3969737987}{5000000000},\qquad
 x=1+\ell-h,\qquad y=h-2\ell.
\end{equation}
Then $\theta=11/12-\ell/4$, $x+y=1-\ell$, and
$0<\ell<x<y<2x$. The exact physical coefficient identity makes the
fixed base character the source principal character outside its finite
bad-prime set. Its moving part is the sextic row, so the preceding
fixed-family proposition applies with the original masks.

Let $d$ now denote a physical row exponent, and let $t=\log_U h_0$
be a free Euler shift. Near the upper row,
\[
 h-10^{-3}\le d\le h+10^{-10},\qquad 0\le t\le10^{-3},
\]
the upper reflected plain length and target slot capacity are
\[
 n(d,t)=1-y/d+t,\qquad z_A(t)=\frac{1-2n(d,t)}C.
\]
A small capacity decrement pays scale-box errors. The exact geometric
checks leave strict room inside
\[
 .41<n(d,t)\le211/500,
 \qquad .03<z_A(t)\le71/2000.
\]
In an amplitude class, let $q=\delta p$ be the average slot gain,
$0\le p\le1/2$. The source selection theorem chooses positive-gain
main slots with actual width $z_I\le z_A$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection}
 |Q_I|^2\ge U^{2qz_A-\delta\,\mathrm{mesh}-o(1)}.
\end{equation}
The gain is measured at the target capacity even if the selected
product is shorter. The total available slot length is $\ell/d$;
the recorded geometry supplies the required capacity.

This distinction resolves the apparent difficulty with the lower
bound $z\ge.03M$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:local}. If $z_I<.03$, the
old moment applies, because
\[
 x_1+x_2+\tfrac92z_I
 \le2(211/500)+\tfrac92(.03)=979/1000<1.
\]
If $z_I\ge.03$ but one actual plain length is below $.41$, the old
capacity instead has reserve $33/4000$. In the remaining case both
plains and the selected width lie in the local window and
$x_1+x_2+Cz_I\le1$. The empty product uses the unrestricted zero-slot
estimate. Thus every case has the same required fourth-moment bound,
with the gain in \eqref{eq:selection} intact (\supp{H}).

The Euler expansion must also cover every free shift. Its reflected
scale and coefficient mass satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass}
 X_u\ll_S(U/Y)h_0,\qquad
 \sum_{h_0,h_r}|c_u(h_0,h_r)|h_0^{\delta/2}\ll U^\eps.
\end{equation}
The repeated-prime factor $h_r$ is already charged in the combined
conductor; its deficit is not charged again. For $t\le10^{-3}$,
$q\le\delta/2$ gives
$-\delta t/2-qz_A(t)/2\le-qz_A(0)/2$.
For larger shifts set $b=2y/d-1$ and
\[
 z_o(t)=\max\{0,(b-2t)/(9/2)\},\qquad
 S_b(t)=\frac t2-\frac{b/C-z_o(t)}4.
\]
The exact audit gives $S_b(10^{-3})>3/10000$. Its slope is $7/18$
until the old capacity vanishes and $1/2$ thereafter. Hence
$-\delta t/2-qz_o(t)/2\le-qz_A(0)/2$ for every larger shift.
At zero capacity, the physical zero-slot moment has no condition on
the sum of the two plain lengths; the actual lengths are each below
$2$, within its source domain. Therefore \eqref{eq:mass} pays every
Euler term. Rows below the local interval retain the old numerator
estimate.

\section{The exact endpoint and continuation}\label{sec:continuation}

Write $\kappa_*=2\theta-1=1874433/2500000$. For the detector parameter
$p\in[0,1/2]$ and zero parameter $\delta\in[0,3/4]$, put
\begin{align*}
 V&=5/6-\delta,&
 D_\kappa&=3-p-2p/(3\kappa),&
 B_\kappa&=2(1-p)(1-p/(3\kappa)),\\
 c_\kappa&=B_\kappa/D_\kappa,&
 R_\kappa&=1-\delta+
 \frac{\delta c_\kappa V}{2(V+\delta c_\kappa)}.
\end{align*}
The physical upper-row exponent is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F}
 F_{C,\kappa}=\frac{1+\ell}{2}+\frac\delta2
 +h\left(\frac{3R_\kappa}4-\frac5{12}\right)
 +\delta p\left(\ell-\frac{h-4\ell}{2C}\right).
\end{equation}
The rational certificate proves on the complete parameter rectangle
\begin{equation}\label{eq:certificate}
 F_{C,\kappa_*}\le\theta-10^{-7}.
\end{equation}
To verify it, set $J=VD_{\kappa_*}+\delta B_{\kappa_*}$.
The audit first proves $J\ge1/10$. Multiplying
$\theta-10^{-7}-F_{C,\kappa_*}$ by $J$ leaves a rational polynomial.
On 18 rational subrectangles all its Bernstein coefficients are
nonnegative, and their union is the whole rectangle. This proves a
continuous inequality, not a numerical-grid observation.
Moreover $P(\ell)>0$ for the quartic $P$ in Section~\ref{sec:setting},
while $P(1/6)<0$ and $P'>0$ on $[1/6,1/5]$. Hence $\ell>\ell_0$
and $\theta<b_0$.

The remaining physical rows require their own checks. The general
exponent is affine in $d$ for $d\ge1/2$. At $\kappa=\kappa_*$ the
certificates give the following savings below $\theta$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
\toprule
Row exponent & Physical coefficient & Saving\\
\midrule
$h$ & $C$ & $10^{-7}$\\
$h-10^{-3}$ & $6\kappa_*$ & $10^{-5}$\\
$1/2$ & $6\kappa_*$ & $10^{-3}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
At the junction the local exponent is no larger than the old one.
Affinity covers the intervening rows, and a slope bound of $2$ pays
the extension to $h+10^{-10}$. Below $1/2$, convexity and an elementary
bound at $d_0=1/200$ cover the retained rows. Floor classes have reserve
exceeding $1/100$; very small rows have a negative exponent relative to the principal scale.
The raw large-row bound pays rows above $h+10^{-10}$ after choosing
its contour parameter. The principal residue, compensated probe,
and off-height tails retain the original contour construction.
The exact inequalities and source domains are recorded in \supp{I}.

It remains to explain why this improves the zero supremum without
assuming the desired conclusion. Let $\beta_*$ be the source Hecke
zero supremum, including the sentinel $1/2$. The incumbent theorem
gives $\beta_*\le7/8$. Suppose $\mu=\beta_*-\theta>0$ and choose
\[
 \kappa=2\beta_*-1,\qquad \kappa_*<\kappa\le3/4.
\]
This meets the old slot-zero hypothesis by equality. For the local
branch, only the detector varies with $\kappa$; the source derivative
bounds give $\partial_\kappa F_{C,\kappa}<1/50$. Thus
\[
 F_{C,2\beta_*-1}-\beta_*
 \le-10^{-7}-\frac{24}{25}\mu.
\]
On the old branches the reciprocal capacity $1/(6\kappa)$ also varies.
Its separate derivative estimate is less than $1/8$, leaving at least
$3\mu/4$ in addition to their certificate reserves. Every retained
class consequently has a common positive saving.

Choose the geometric margins, capacity decrement, amplitude mesh, and
finite slot system before fixing a primitive target $\eta$. Their
smallest slot fixes the principal prime-product saving. For that target,
now choose the finite dictionary and profile orders, then a sufficiently
small analysis-height exponent, and finally the tail order and eventual
threshold. The constants and threshold may depend on $\eta$; the
resulting positive exponent $\Delta$ does not. Taking $\Delta<\mu/4$
and smaller than all the preceding reserves gives
\[
 J_\eta(Z)-f_\eta(Z)\ll_\eta Z^{\beta_*-k-\Delta},
 \qquad k=5/6-x/3-\ell/6.
\]
The compensated principal bound is
$J_\eta(Z)\ll_\eta Z^{\theta-k+\eps}$, so the same improved exponent
bounds $f_\eta$. Its Mellin transform is holomorphic in
$\Re s>\beta_*-\Delta$ and, originally in $\Re s>1$, equals
\[
 \int_0^\infty f_\eta(Z)Z^{-s+k}\,\frac{dZ}{Z}
 =e^{(s-5/6)^2}\frac{H_\eta(s)}{L^S(s,\eta)}.
\]
The source cutoff has $|H_\eta-1|\le1/2$ in $\Re s>\theta$.
Holomorphic continuation therefore excludes zeros to the right of
$\beta_*-\Delta$. Since $\Delta$ is target-independent, choose a
primitive zero sufficiently near the supremum and then use its target's
estimate: this contradicts the definition of $\beta_*$. Thus
$\beta_*\le\theta$.

Fixed Euler factors restore all imprimitive Hecke presentations without
adding zeros in this positive half-plane. For an ordinary Dirichlet
character $\chi$, its Eisenstein norm lift satisfies
\[
 L_K(s,\eta_\chi)=L(s,\chi)L(s,\chi\chi_{-3}).
\]
In $\theta<\Re s<1$ the product is nonzero and both factors are finite,
so $L(s,\chi)\ne0$. Classical nonvanishing covers $\Re s\ge1$ with
the principal-pole exception. The modulus-one character gives the zeta
case and completes the argument for Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint}.

\section{Proof record and attribution}\label{sec:credits}

The full proof is retained in the separate supplement: B identifies the
signed rows and masks; C fixes the character dictionary; D proves the
unmarked input; E gives uniform Fourier transport; F treats actual
restricted children and radii; G assembles the local moment; H handles
shortened scales and slots; I proves the endpoint and continuation.
The original exact audits and the additional 20 rational endpoint checks
are included in the accompanying source package~\cite{gao}.

The analytic argument is proposed relative to the pinned framework.
Supporting development checks compile 15 Lean components and an audit
module using cached dependencies, with 48 printed axiom checks containing
only the standard axioms. A complete formal endpoint theorem, a clean
full-dependency replay, canonical-target comparison, and independent
three-kernel verification remain to be established. The finite certificates
and component checks have the precise scopes recorded in the package.

OpenAI supplies the initial $7/8$ theorem and analytic-arithmetic
framework; Levy supplies the weighted-numerator input; Collet supplies
the variable moment, detector, quartic optimization, and source
architecture. The contribution proposed here is the local $4.498$
refinement, its joint-label analysis, and the resulting endpoint assembly.
Earlier reconstruction and unmarked-input work is recorded in
\cite{gaoreconstruction}. The package retains Apache-2.0 licensing and
upstream notices; attribution does not imply endorsement.
